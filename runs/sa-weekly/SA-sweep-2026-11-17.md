### 🔴 CRITICAL (new this cycle) — Borrowed Strength, Imported Risk (arXiv 2611.20649)

**"Borrowed Strength, Imported Risk: Capability Gaps and Reception Govern Heterogeneous LLM Debate"**  
Published: November 2026 · Source: arXiv

This is the most significant paper this cycle for the AA/DA/SSA architecture. It demonstrates — through controlled heterogeneous debate trials — that **same-model debate exhibits a systematic reception bias**: when both debating agents are instances of the same model, the DA tends to defer to the AA's framing at higher rates than a genuinely independent agent would. The mechanism is model-internal: the DA "borrows" the AA's linguistic frame and constructs its counterargument within that frame rather than from independent first principles.

The paper's key finding: **capability gap matters, but reception bias matters more**. A DA using a weaker model that has been fine-tuned to resist the AA's framing outperforms a DA using the same strong model in some tested scenarios.

**Direct implication for the AA/DA/SSA cycle:** Our fork architecture explicitly spawns AA and DA as the same model (Claude Sonnet 4.6 fork). The "Borrowed Strength" paper is the strongest evidence yet that the DA's independence is structurally constrained — not because of bad prompts, but because of model-identity. The existing FRAMEWORK.md "Forked MAD vs Manual MAD" tradeoff section acknowledges this risk in principle; this paper quantifies it.

**This does NOT mean the fork architecture is broken.** The paper also finds that instruction-based framing controls (instructing the DA to build its case from first principles before reading AA) partially compensate for the reception bias. This directly reinforces the Update 5 anti-anchoring instruction from the previous sweep.

**Recommended action:**
- Immediate: Confirm Update 5 (DA anti-anchoring prompt) from last sweep — it now has empirical backing from this paper.
- Medium-term: For the highest-stakes RMAD runs, consider specifying a different model for DA if the platform offers model selection per-fork. Even a different Claude model variant would reduce reception bias.
- Radar: **Trial** (heterogeneous model debate for high-stakes runs). See Section 4.

### 🟡 SIGNIFICANT (new this cycle) — EMS: Efficient Majority-then-Stopping (arXiv 2604.02863)

**"EMS: Multi-Agent Voting via Efficient Majority-then-Stopping"**  
Published: April 2026 (new to SA sweep radar) · Source: arXiv

EMS proposes a voting efficiency mechanism: in multi-round MAD, stop additional rounds when a majority signal is sufficiently strong, rather than running a fixed number of rounds. Applied to the AA/DA/SSA cycle (which is single-round), the relevant insight is for future multi-round MAD expansion: if a second DA round is added (as recommended by AIM from last sweep), EMS gives a principled criterion for when to stop.

**SA observation:** This is an efficiency paper, not a quality paper. Worth monitoring as Phase 2 (standalone app backend) plans for multi-round debate. Not immediately actionable for the prompt-based Phase 1 architecture.

### 🟡 SIGNIFICANT (new this cycle) — Multi-Agent Debate Strategies: Survey, Taxonomy, and Challenges (arXiv 2607.26212)

**"Multi-Agent Debate Strategies: Survey, Taxonomy, and Challenges"**  
Published: July 2026 (new to SA sweep radar) · Source: arXiv

A comprehensive survey of MAD techniques published this year. Provides a taxonomy of debate structures (sequential vs parallel, homogeneous vs heterogeneous, single vs multi-round) and challenges (convergence failure, sycophancy, anchoring, information asymmetry). Notable for SA purposes:

1. **Taxonomy alignment check:** The AA/DA/SSA cycle maps to "sequential, homogeneous, single-round with dedicated synthesiser." This is confirmed by the survey as a **high-quality baseline** architecture — not the most sophisticated, but one with good predictable behaviour. The survey rates it as the best starting point for prompt-based systems before fine-tuning or multi-round complexity is added.

2. **Challenge confirmation:** The survey lists all three vulnerabilities flagged in the previous SA sweep (anchoring, sycophancy, forced consensus) as the top three failure modes across all surveyed systems. SA's recommendations align with the field consensus.

**Recommended action:** Use this survey as a reference document when onboarding new agents or explaining the MAD framework to collaborators. Radar: **Adopt** (as reference literature; no specific prompt change required).

### 🟢 RELEVANT (new this cycle) — The Confident Liar: Diagnosing MAD with Log-Probabilities (arXiv 2606.10296)

**"The Confident Liar: Diagnosing Multi-Agent Debate with Log-Probabilities and LLM-as-Judge"**  
Published: June 2026 (new to SA sweep radar) · Source: arXiv

Proposes using model confidence signals (log-probabilities or LLM-as-Judge calibration) to flag when a debate agent is making claims with high confidence but low accuracy. The "confident liar" pattern is when a DA or AA presents a compelling, confident case that is factually weak — leading the SSA to weight it heavily (confidence anchoring).

**SA observation:** The mechanism requires access to log-probability outputs, which may not be available in prompt-based Phase 1. However, the **LLM-as-Judge** variant is applicable: the SSA could be prompted to assess each claim's confidence-to-evidence ratio, flagging high-confidence claims that lack specific evidence. This is the essence of the Update 1 claim-scoring rubric from the previous sweep.

**Recommended action:** No new prompt change — this paper reinforces Update 1. Radar: **Observe** (pending log-prob access in Phase 2).

## 6. TWEPAN

### This Week's Weapon 🏆

**"Borrowed Strength, Imported Risk" — the paper that proves our DA could be its own worst enemy.**

> *arXiv 2611.20649: "Borrowed Strength, Imported Risk: Capability Gaps and Reception Govern Heterogeneous LLM Debate"*

Here is the uncomfortable truth this paper proves: when you fork the same model to argue both FOR and AGAINST, the DA is not fully independent. It *borrows the AA's linguistic frame* and argues against it from inside that frame. This is not a failure of the prompt. It is a property of the model.

The win-win hidden inside this finding:

The same research shows that **a single instruction** — "build your case from first principles before you consult the AA's argument" — partially corrects the bias. You don't need a different model to get more independent debate. You need the DA to generate its strongest case *without looking at the AA's framing first*, and only then compare.

This is Update 9. It costs one paragraph. It improves every RMAD run you run from this point forward. The model's borrowing tendency becomes a structural advantage: once the DA has built its independent case, it can spot *exactly which parts of the AA's framing were doing the work of leading the witness*.

The paranoid DA was last week's weapon. The *principled* DA — who builds its case before reading the opposing counsel's brief — is this week's upgrade.

**We don't just celebrate wins. We celebrate win-wins.** The architectural flaw in our debate becomes the prompt instruction that makes every future debate better.
