## 1. New MAD Research Findings

The following papers and resources surfaced in the past 7–14 days. All are directly relevant to the AA/DA/SSA architecture.

### 🔴 CRITICAL — Debate to Deceive (arXiv 2611.08756)

**"Debate to Deceive: Poisoning LLM's Fact-Checking via Game-Theoretic Multi-Agent Debate"**  
Published: November 2026 · Source: arXiv

This paper is the most consequential MAD research released this cycle. It demonstrates — through formal game-theoretic analysis and empirical testing — that a single adversarial agent inside a MAD pipeline can systematically manipulate the SSA verdict by strategically selecting *which true facts to present* and *which to withhold*. The attack requires no jailbreak, no hallucination, and no access to the other agent's output. It operates purely through selective framing.

**Direct implication for the AA/DA/SSA cycle:** Our fork-based architecture has the DA receive AA's output before forming its response. The "Debate to Deceive" attack model is precisely the reverse scenario (a corrupted AA anchoring the DA), but the mechanism is symmetric: *any agent that sees the other's output before responding is vulnerable to anchoring manipulation*. This is not a reason to abandon fork-based MAD (see FRAMEWORK.md's "Forked MAD vs Manual MAD — Documented Tradeoff"), but it is a reason to add explicit anti-anchoring instructions to both AA and DA prompts.

**Recommended action:** See Section 3 (Prompt Updates) for specific wording.

### 🟡 SIGNIFICANT — AIM: Adaptive Interaction in Multi-Agent Debate (arXiv 2611.08950)

**"AIM: Adaptive Interaction in Multi-Agent Debate for Multimodal LLM Inference"**  
Published: November 2026 · Source: arXiv

AIM proposes that agents in a debate should adapt *how much* they interact based on confidence signals — terminating debate rounds early when agents converge and extending when divergence persists. Applied to the AA/DA/SSA cycle: the SSA could be instructed to call for a second DA round if the first DA's objections are assessed as low-confidence or superficial. This directly addresses a known gap: some SSA outputs (observed in lighter debate runs) synthesise agreement too quickly when DA's case is thin.

**Recommended action:** Add SSA escalation instruction — Radar: **Observe → Trial**.

---

### 🟡 SIGNIFICANT — Learning to Adjudicate: Verifier-Guided Multi-Agent Reasoning (arXiv 2611.02758)

**"Learning to Adjudicate: Verifier-Guided Multi-Agent Reasoning for E-Commerce Relevance"**  
Published: November 2026 · Source: arXiv

Introduces a structured verifier step in the SSA role: before synthesising, the adjudicator scores each AA and DA claim on (a) evidence quality, (b) logical consistency, and (c) relevance to the decision. The SSA verdict is then explicitly tied to scored claims rather than holistic judgment. This formalises what strong SSA outputs (like `traverse-ilogic-audit`) already do informally.

**Recommended action:** Add explicit claim-scoring rubric to SSA prompt — Radar: **Trial**.

---

### 🟢 RELEVANT — Diverse Thinking: Breaking Binary Opposition in Debates (EMNLP 2026 Findings)

**"Diverse Thinking: Breaking Oppositions in Debates to Foster Synergized Reasoning via Multi-Agent Collaboration"**  
Published: EMNLP 2026 Findings

Argues that strictly binary (FOR vs AGAINST) debate formats suppress the most valuable reasoning — the third-path "win-win" synthesis. Proposes a third agent role (Integrator) that runs in parallel with AA and DA, explicitly seeking non-obvious solutions that neither the AA nor DA advocated. This is structurally consonant with the existing SSA role, but adds the insight that "win-wins" should be *generated* as a parallel track, not *discovered* retroactively by synthesis.

**Recommended action:** Add "Win-Win Generator" sub-task to SSA prompt — Radar: **Adopt** (this aligns with existing FRAMEWORK.md TWEPAN philosophy of celebrating win-wins).

---

### 🟢 RELEVANT — Free-MAD: Consensus-Free Multi-Agent Debate (arXiv 2509.11035)

**"Free-MAD: Consensus-Free Multi-Agent Debate"**  
Published: September 2026 · Source: arXiv

Challenges the assumption that debate should converge. Proposes that forcing agents to reach consensus degrades output quality — the SSA should preserve genuine dissent rather than paper over it. For our framework, this reinforces the existing SSA practice of issuing a "REJECTED" verdict (as seen in `traverse-ilogic-audit` DA #12) rather than finding a forced middle ground.

**Recommended action:** Explicitly add "do not force agreement — issue a REJECTED verdict when DA's claim is not supported" to SSA prompt — Radar: **Adopt** (already practised; needs codification).

---

### 🟡 SIGNIFICANT — MADBench: Security Benchmarking for Multi-Agent Debate (arXiv 2609.39146)

**"MADBench: Benchmarking the Security of Multi-Agent Debate"**  
Published: September 2026 · Source: arXiv

A benchmarking suite specifically for evaluating MAD systems' resilience to adversarial manipulation. Directly complements the "Debate to Deceive" paper. Provides a reproducible test harness for assessing whether SSA outputs can be manipulated through biased AA/DA framing.

**Recommended action:** Worth monitoring as a future evaluation tool for high-stakes MAD runs — Radar: **Observe**.

---

### 🟡 RELEVANT — When Most Models Are Wrong: Anatomy of MAD Failure (Blog, October 2026)

**"When Most Models Are Wrong: An Anatomy of Multi-Agent Debate Failure — and a Rational Rebuild"**  
Published: October 2026 · Source: leiwang84.com

A practitioner-level post-mortem on MAD failure modes. Key finding: the most common MAD failure is not factual error — it is *structural sycophancy*, where the SSA defaults to agreement with the majority (or the more forceful presenter) rather than the more correct argument. The proposed fix is explicit "majority-is-wrong" test: SSA should ask itself "if both AA and DA agreed on X, would that make X true?" — and treat convergence as a reason to scrutinise harder, not less.

**Recommended action:** Add anti-sycophancy instruction to SSA prompt — Radar: **Adopt**.

### Pattern 3 — SSA Quality Variance: Depth Correlated with Domain Complexity

**Severity:** Informational · **Alert type:** ADVANCEMENT

The `traverse-ilogic-audit` SSA output is exceptional — a 12-claim verdict table with CONFIRMED/REJECTED rulings, 8 prioritised fix specifications with working code, and 4 win-win enhancements. The `mad-automation-architecture` SSA is appropriately strategic but more compressed.

This quality variance is expected and healthy — different question types warrant different depth. However, it surfaces a gap: there is no explicit SSA depth calibration instruction. Without one, SSA quality depends on the AI's implicit judgment of how much depth the topic warrants.

**SA recommendation:** Add a "depth calibration signal" to the SSA prompt: *"If AA or DA produced a detailed evidence-backed argument (with code, data, or citations), your synthesis must match that depth. If both produced high-level arguments, a strategic synthesis is appropriate. Never compress a detailed debate into a brief verdict."*

## 3. Recommended Prompt and Protocol Updates

The following changes are recommended for the AA, DA, and SSA prompts. These are proposals — Waynito retains final authority on all protocol changes (FRAMEWORK.md Principle 7: "Human judgement remains the ultimate authority").

---

### Update 1 — SSA Prompt: Explicit Claim-Scoring Rubric (from "Learning to Adjudicate")

**Add before the verdict table section:**

```
Before writing the verdict table, complete a silent scoring pass:
For each AA and DA claim, assess:
  - Evidence quality: Is this supported by specific evidence (code, data, citation) or assertion?
  - Logical consistency: Does the claim follow from the stated premises?
  - Relevance: Does this claim materially affect the decision?

A claim scored LOW on evidence quality cannot earn "CONFIRMED" — it earns "PLAUSIBLE" at most.
A claim scored LOW on relevance should be noted but not drive the verdict.
```

---

### Update 2 — SSA Prompt: Anti-Sycophancy Instruction (from "MAD Failure Anatomy" blog)

**Add to SSA framing section:**

```
Anti-sycophancy rule: If AA and DA converge on the same conclusion, treat this as a reason
to scrutinise harder, not less. Convergence in a debate does not equal correctness.
Ask: "If both are wrong in the same direction, what does the evidence actually say?"
```

---

### Update 3 — SSA Prompt: Explicit REJECTED Verdict Instruction (from Free-MAD)

**Add to verdict instructions:**

```
Do not force agreement. If a DA claim is not supported by evidence after scrutiny,
issue a REJECTED verdict with explanation. A forced middle-ground verdict that
papers over a genuinely unfounded claim is worse than a clear rejection.
```

---

### Update 4 — SSA Prompt: Win-Win as a Parallel Track (from "Diverse Thinking" EMNLP 2026)

**Add a dedicated Win-Win section request:**

```
Part 3 of your output must be Win-Win Enhancements — improvements that neither AA nor DA
explicitly advocated, that make the outcome better for all parties. These are not
compromises. They are third-path solutions. Minimum: 2 win-win enhancements per run.
```

*(Note: This is already practised in strong SSA outputs like traverse-ilogic-audit. This update codifies it as mandatory.)*

---

### Update 5 — DA Prompt: Anti-Anchoring Instruction (from "Debate to Deceive")

**Add to DA framing section:**

```
Important: You have received the AA's output. Do not let it anchor your analysis.
Treat the AA's framing as potentially adversarial — constructed to emphasise evidence
that favours its case and minimise evidence that doesn't.
Your job is not to rebut the AA's specific claims. Your job is to build the strongest
independent case AGAINST the proposition from first principles, then compare it to what
AA actually argued.
```

---

### Update 6 — AA Prompt: Tool-Use Directive (from Tool-MAD research)

**Add to AA framing section:**

```
Where the proposition involves factual claims (current state of a technology,
published research, market data, pricing, compatibility), use web search to
verify and cite your evidence. An asserted AA case is weaker than a
cited AA case. Prioritise recency: a 2024 paper does not supersede 2026 practice.
```

---

### Update 7 — SSA Prompt: Depth Calibration Signal (from Pattern 3 observation)

**Add to SSA framing section:**

```
Depth calibration: Match your synthesis depth to the debate's depth.
- If AA or DA produced code, formulas, or citations: your synthesis must address each
  specific claim with matching specificity.
- If both produced high-level strategic arguments: a structured strategic verdict is
  appropriate.
Never compress a detailed debate into a brief verdict.
```

---

### Update 8 — PLU-MAD-protocol.md: Filename Convention Enforcement (from Pattern 2 observation)

**Add to the file convention section:**

```
Run output filenames are FIXED: AA-output.md, DA-output.md, SSA-output.md, README.md
"AA-brief.md" and "DA-brief.md" are not valid alternatives.
If a run was committed with non-standard names, it is acceptable to leave them
in place (do not rename historical runs). All new runs must use the standard names.
```
