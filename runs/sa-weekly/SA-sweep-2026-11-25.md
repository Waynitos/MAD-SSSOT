### 🔴 CRITICAL (new this cycle) — AIM: Adaptive Interaction in Multi-Agent Debate (arXiv 2611.08950, NeurIPS 2026)

**"AIM: Adaptive Interaction in Multi-Agent Debate for Multimodal LLM Inference"**  
Published: November 2026 · Venue: NeurIPS 2026 Poster · Source: [arXiv 2611.08950](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08950)

AIM is the most significant new paper this week. It proposes a **dynamic switching mechanism** between two interaction modes in multi-agent debate:

- **Full interaction mode**: All agents exchange full responses each round (standard MAD). Used when confidence is low or arguments are divergent.
- **Partial interaction mode**: Agents receive only summaries or key claims from other agents (not full responses). Used when confidence is high or convergence is already strong.

The key finding: AIM's adaptive switching achieves **comparable accuracy to full-interaction MAD at substantially lower token cost** — and in multimodal settings (image + text), it outperforms full-interaction on tasks where visual evidence is contested, because partial-mode prevents agents from anchoring on each other's visual interpretations.

**Direct implication for the AA/DA/SSA cycle:**

The current MAD protocol has AA and DA each reading the full output of the other (via SSA). AIM's finding suggests a **structural improvement for high-stakes RMAD runs**:

- In the DA phase, partial interaction mode means the DA should receive only the AA's *core claim and key evidence* — not the full rhetorical argument. This is a mechanistic version of Update 9 (received-frame resistance). The DA forms its case against the *claim*, not the *framing*.
- In the SSA phase, full interaction is appropriate — the synthesiser should read both full arguments to weight them.

This is the first NeurIPS-level paper this sweep cycle to directly validate a specific structural change to our AA/DA/SSA protocol.

**Recommended action:**
- **Update 11 (new):** Add a "DA reduced-context" instruction to the DA prompt: "You will receive the AA's core claim and top three evidence points only. Build your case against the claim — do not attempt to access or reconstruct AA's full argument."
- Radar: **Trial** (adaptive interaction mode / DA reduced-context)

### 🔴 SIGNIFICANT (new this cycle) — Debate to Deceive: Adversarial MAD Poisoning (arXiv 2611.08756)

**"Debate to Deceive: Poisoning LLM's Fact-Checking via Game-Theoretic Multi-Agent Debate"**  
Published: November 2026 · Source: [arXiv 2611.08756](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08756)

This paper demonstrates that multi-agent debate systems can be systematically poisoned by an adversarial agent that uses **game-theoretic strategies** to manipulate the SSA's verdict. The attack works by:

1. The adversarial agent (acting as AA or DA) deliberately frames claims in a way that is *superficially persuasive but factually weak*.
2. The adversarial agent then *concedes selectively* on less important points to signal apparent good faith to the SSA.
3. The SSA, interpreting selective concession as evidence quality, weights the adversarial agent's remaining claims higher.

The paper calls this the **"strategic concession" attack** — it is not random noise. It is a deliberate exploitation of SSA's default assumption that a debater who concedes some points is probably right on the ones they don't concede.

**Direct implication for the AA/DA/SSA cycle:**

In our current architecture, neither the AA nor the DA is adversarial in intent — but the *structural conditions* for strategic concession still apply:
- If the DA (or AA) finds its weakest arguments under pressure and concedes them first, the SSA will weight remaining claims higher.
- Update 8 ("SSA flag when DA concedes without evidence") directly addresses this — but it's still unimplemented.
- **Update 12 (new):** Add a SSA instruction: "Treat any point conceded by one debater as UNDECIDED — not as evidence that the conceding agent is right on remaining points. Each claim stands or falls on its own evidence, regardless of concession patterns."

**Radar:** **Adopt** as a known attack vector — SSA prompts must explicitly defend against strategic concession.

### 🟡 SIGNIFICANT (new this cycle) — Confidence-Guided Adaptive Debate (ICML 2026)

**"Efficient Multi-Agent Reasoning via Confidence-Guided Adaptive Debate"**  
Published: 2026 · Venue: ICML 2026 · Source: [proceedings.mlr.press](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/yoa26a.html)

Complementary to AIM, this ICML paper proposes a **confidence threshold** to decide when to trigger debate at all. The key finding: for tasks where model confidence (measured by response consistency across runs) exceeds a threshold, debate adds noise rather than signal — single-pass answers outperform MAD.

**SA observation:** This has direct relevance to the **Mini-MAD vs RMAD routing decision** in PLU's Layer 2. The current Mini-MAD trigger watches for 5 linguistic patterns. This paper suggests an additional quantitative criterion: how uncertain/contested is this topic? If a decision has obvious, low-stakes answers, even Mini-MAD may be overkill.

**Recommended action:** No prompt change — this reinforces the existing three-layer routing (RMAD / Mini-MAD / FTCA threshold) design. RMAD should continue to be used only for genuinely contested, high-stakes decisions. Radar: **Observe**.

Published: March 2026 (new to SA sweep) · Source: [arXiv 2603.20640](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20640)

## 6. TWEPAN 🏆

### This Week's Weapon

**AIM's insight: the debate interaction mode itself is a variable — not a constant.**

> *NeurIPS 2026 · "AIM: Adaptive Interaction in Multi-Agent Debate for Multimodal LLM Inference" · [arXiv 2611.08950](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08950)*

Every MAD framework — ours included — treats the debate structure as fixed: AA argues, DA argues, SSA adjudicates. What AIM proves is that **the amount each agent sees of the other is itself a design choice** with real consequences for output quality and cost.

Here is the win-win hiding in this paper:

The weakest point in our AA/DA/SSA cycle has always been DA independence. The DA reads the AA's full argument and must resist its framing. Update 5 (anti-anchoring), Update 9 (received-frame resistance), and Update 11 (reduced-context mode) all attack the same vulnerability from different angles. AIM provides the mechanistic explanation: *full interaction propagates framing bias*. Partial interaction — giving the DA only the AA's claim, not its rhetoric — structurally limits the bias regardless of prompt instructions.

The same fix that makes our debate cheaper also makes it more honest. The DA doesn't resist the AA's framing because we told it to. It resists because it never saw the framing in full.

This is not "less debate." This is better debate: *a DA that argues from its own first principles, not from a reaction to an opponent's script.*

**We don't just celebrate wins. We celebrate win-wins.**  
The "weakness" (DA reads too much of AA) becomes the design handle: strip it back. The DA becomes more independent, the SSA adjudicates more honestly, and the whole run uses fewer tokens.
